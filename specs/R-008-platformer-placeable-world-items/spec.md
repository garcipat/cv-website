# Feature Specification: Platformer Placeable World Items

**Feature Branch**: `R-008-platformer-placeable-world-items`

**Created**: 2026-09-26

**Status**: Draft

**Input**: GitHub issue #96 — "R-008: Platformer Placeable World Items". Give the level's player-affected objects one umbrella registry with two subtypes: `SpawnedType<S>` (the player spawns it at runtime — today the placed bomb) and `WorldInteractableType<S>` (placed in the level and activated by the player — today the rope ladder and the chest), both extending a shared `DeployableItemType<S>` base backed by one live collection, one tick, one draw dispatch and one interaction dispatch; consolidate the bomb concept into one home exposing its held and placed faces; fold the chest into the world-interactable family (resolving finding X7); and keep the shipped landing-row helper as the one downward scan.

**Depends on**: [R-001 Platformer Core Contracts & Dependency Layers](../R-001-platformer-core-contracts/spec.md) (shipped: the `contracts/` layer and its layer invariants — `contracts/` a leaf, no `level/ → engine/`, no `engine/ → state/`), [R-002 Platformer Shared Primitives & Dedup](../R-002-platformer-shared-primitives-dedup/spec.md) (shipped: `Standable.ts`'s shared `findLandingRow` with all three callers routed through it — the issue's P3; and the pickup-constant/co-location cleanups), [R-006 Platformer Pickup Unification](../R-006-platformer-pickup-unification/spec.md) (shipped: the shared `Pickup` base, the `PICKUP_TYPES` registry with `spawn`/`onPickup`, one `checkPickupCollisions` and one `drawPickups` with per-kind `drawLayer`, and the per-kind `pickupStores`), [R-007 Platformer Registry Dispatch Completion](../R-007-platformer-registry-dispatch-completion/spec.md) (shipped: `EnemyType.onDefeat`, `HazardType.knocksBack`/`withTickState`, and the `PickupKind | null` held-item vocabulary), [O-011 Platformer Deployable Ladders](../O-011-platformer-deployable-ladders/spec.md) (shipped as the `DeployableLadder` state machine — renamed to `RopeLadder*` by this feature — plus its markers and its effective-terrain application), [O-012 Platformer Bombs](../O-012-platformer-bombs/spec.md) (shipped: the placed-bomb state machine, the dropped bomb pickup and the bomb-pot drop), and [S-007 Platformer Chests & Completion](../S-007-platformer-chests/spec.md) (shipped: the chest state, `CHEST_TYPE`, and the open/completion behaviour).

**Pending dependency**: [R-015 Platformer Tile Module Registry](https://github.com/garcipat/cv-website/issues/111). The rope ladder's rolled `ladderBundle` is a stateless tile until deployed, and R-015 owns the stateless-tile contract. R-008 MUST NOT build a second tile registry; it owns only the live-object end and consumes R-015's tile contract where the two meet (analysis §3.5, Phase 6).

**Design reference**: [`docs/PlatformerArchitectureAnalysis.md`](../../docs/PlatformerArchitectureAnalysis.md) — Phase 6, findings **P1** (`PlacedBomb` and `DeployableLadder` are unrelated bespoke implementations with no generic placeable), **P2** (the bomb concept is spread across `PlacedBomb.ts`, `pickups/Bomb.ts` and the folded `BombPickup.ts`), **P3** ("first landing row below" implemented three times — already consolidated by R-002 and only preserved here), and **X7** (`CHEST_TYPE` is a degenerate one-member family with two import paths); plus the taxonomy in §3.5 (`DeployableItemType<S>` is the umbrella over the load-placed interactables and the runtime-spawned items) and the target tree in §4.1.

## Clarifications

### Session 2026-09-26

- Q: Should `DeployableItemType<S>` compose the existing `WorldType<S>` contract (`contracts/WorldType.ts`) and join the `WorldType` conformance suite? → A: Yes — compose `WorldType<S>` (reusing its `key`/`draw`) and keep `box` an optional extra, NOT the required `Boxed<S>`; add `DEPLOYABLE_ITEM_TYPES` to the `entities/WorldType.test.ts` conformance suite.
- Q: Should `DeployableItemType<S>` carry a `sprite: SpriteDescriptor` and have `DEPLOYABLE_ITEM_TYPES` added to the page's `collectSheetSources` discovery list? → A: Yes — carry a primary `sprite` and walk the registry, so a new deployable-item kind with a new sheet needs no page `loadImage` edit.
- Q: Where in the frame should the one shared deployable-items tick run, given the rope ladder advances before player physics and the bomb after it? → A: Two-phase — advance every kind's `step` in one `tickDeployableItems` at the rope ladder's early (pre-physics) site, then apply each kind's own consequence hook at the bomb's late (post-physics, pre-persist) site.
- Q: What should the family and the rope ladder be named, given `WorldItem` collides conceptually with the shipped `WorldType` (which already covers pickups/enemies/blocks/chests) and a bomb is placed while a ladder is deployed? → A: Name the family `DeployableItem*` (`DeployableItemType` / `DeployableItemState` / `DeployableItemKind` / `DEPLOYABLE_ITEM_TYPES` / `deployableItems` / `entities/deployableItems/`), and rename the ladder's shipped `DeployableLadder*` vocabulary to `RopeLadder*` (state, phase, module and helpers) so the two no longer share the `Deployable` prefix.
- Q: The bomb is the only deployable item that can explode — should its blast be hidden from the shared contract? → A: No — keep the small declarative `blasts` field on `DeployableItemOutcome`. The blast decision/geometry already live in the bomb module and `engine/Blast.ts`; the shared layer carries only an event the one page applier resolves, mirroring `PickupOutcome`'s single-kind fields (`heal`/`bombs`/`bankKey`). Performing the blast in the bomb module is impossible without an imperative capability (the rejected leak) or an `entities/ → state/` edge (FR-013); the full damage subsystem is R-011's `BombSystem`.
- Q: Should the umbrella be split into subtypes by how an object comes into play? → A: Yes — `DeployableItemType<S>` is the umbrella; `SpawnedType<S>` is for objects the player spawns at runtime (today the bomb) and `WorldInteractableType<S>` is for objects placed in the level that the player activates (today the rope ladder and the chest). The rope ladder is author-seeded like the chest, so it belongs with the chest; the bomb is the only runtime-spawned kind.
- Q: How far should the chest fold into the world-interactable family? → A: Full fold — the chest becomes a `WorldInteractableType` entry, its Up-press open, key cost and fact reveal travel the same declarative `onPlayerInteract` outcome as the ladder's deploy, and the page names no chest kind. This resolves finding X7 by folding rather than by leaving a one-member family.

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - Player-affected objects share one umbrella, one collection, one tick, one draw and one interaction dispatch (Priority: P1)

Today three player-affected objects are wired by hand, each by name: a placed bomb (`placedBombs` + `stepPlacedBomb` + `drawPlacedBombs`), a rope ladder bundle (`deployableLadderStates` + `tickDeployableLadders` + `drawDeployableLadders`), and a chest (`chestStates` + a page-side open branch + `drawChests`). The bomb and ladder are near-identical in shape — `id`, `col`, `row`, tile-aligned `x`/`y`, a lifecycle timer, a `step`, a bespoke renderer — and the ladder and chest are both author-placed objects the player activates with the same Up press. After this feature all three are entries in one `DeployableItemType<S>` umbrella registry backed by one live `deployableItems` collection, advanced by one tick, drawn by one dispatch pass and activated by one interaction dispatch. The umbrella has two subtypes: `SpawnedType<S>` (the player spawns it at runtime — the bomb) and `WorldInteractableType<S>` (it is placed in the level and the player activates it — the rope ladder and the chest). The page names no kind in its tick, draw or interaction path, and a new object is one module plus one registry line.

**Why this priority**: This is the central finding the issue names (P1) and the "no placeable-item registry" gap: it is the prerequisite for every future object of either subtype, and it is the same registry-plus-per-kind-module shape blocks, enemies, hazards, chests and pickups already use.

**Independent Test**: Search `PlatformerPage.tsx` for `placedBombs`, `deployableLadderStates`, `chestStates`, `stepPlacedBomb`, `tickDeployableLadders`, `drawPlacedBombs`, `drawDeployableLadders` and `drawChests` — the separate live arrays and the hand-wired page passes are gone, replaced by one `DeployableItemType` umbrella registry, one `deployableItems` collection, one tick, one `drawDeployableItems` dispatch and one shared interaction dispatch that forwards each entry to its kind's own `step`/`draw`/`onPlayerInteract`. Confirm the page's tick, draw and interaction paths name no deployable-item kind.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the deployable-item registry, **When** its entries are inspected, **Then** each entry carries the shared base (a state with `id`/`col`/`row`/world `x`/`y`/`kind`, a primary `sprite` descriptor, a draw layer, an optional `box(state)`, a `resetScope` and a `draw(state, dc)`), the bomb is a `SpawnedType` entry and the rope ladder and chest are `WorldInteractableType` entries, and each entry's kind matches its state discriminator.
2. **Given** one or more live objects, **When** the playing tick runs, **Then** one shared tick advances every entry that has a `step` exactly as the current paths do (the bomb's fuse always advances and gravity pulls it to its landing row while falling; the ladder's unroll advances only while `deploying`; the chest has no `step`), with no per-kind branch in the page.
3. **Given** the render loop, **When** objects are drawn, **Then** one `drawDeployableItems` dispatch forwards each entry to its kind's own `draw`, invoked at the draw bands needed to preserve today's order (the ladder's terrain-level bundle/shaft band, the bomb's after-blocks band and the chest's band), and no kind is named by the caller.
4. **Given** an Up press, **When** the shared interaction dispatch runs, **Then** it offers the press to the interactable entries in the order that preserves today's precedence (a rolled rope-ladder bundle under the player deploys before a chest it is standing on can open), applies the first outcome through the generic applier, and the page names no kind.
5. **Given** a brand-new kind, **When** it ships, **Then** it is one module plus one registry line with no edit to the page's tick/draw/interaction dispatch or `Renderer.ts`.

### User Story 2 - The bomb concept lives in one home with its held and placed faces (Priority: P1)

The bomb is spread across files today: `engine/PlacedBomb.ts` owns the placed state machine, fuse frames, fall and landing rules; `entities/pickups/Bomb.ts` owns the dropped-pickup `PickupType` view and its drawing; the former `entities/BombPickup.ts` constants module has already been folded into the view — and the pot drop (`bombPot.drop: 'bomb'`) routes through the pickup registry. Yet the pickup view explicitly points at the placed module for "the source of truth for every constant", so the fuse/frame logic is not reachable from the pickup view even though both draw `bomb.png`. After this feature a bomb has one home exposing both its **held/pickup face** (the `PICKUP_TYPES.bomb` view the player collects) and its **spawned/placed face** (the `SpawnedType<PlacedBombState>` the player lights), sharing one set of constants and one sprite so the two cannot drift.

**Why this priority**: This is the issue's P2 and the direct "consolidate the bomb concept into one home with held + placed faces" instruction. It also proves the per-kind-module pattern for a concept that legitimately has two runtime faces.

**Independent Test**: Search the theme for the bomb's constants and state — there is exactly one bomb module home owning the placed state machine, the fuse-frame mapping, the landing predicate, the held `PickupType` and the spawned `SpawnedType` entry; `pickups/Bomb.ts`'s "the other file is the source of truth" cross-reference is gone because there is no other file. Confirm the placed bomb still falls, lights and detonates and the dropped bomb still bobs and is collected exactly as today.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the bomb module, **When** it is inspected, **Then** it owns both faces — the held face registered as `PICKUP_TYPES.bomb` and the spawned face registered as the bomb `SpawnedType` — plus the fuse frames, fuse timing, gravity/landing rules and shared sprite constants, with no second bomb module.
2. **Given** a destroyed bomb pot, **When** it drops, **Then** the drop still routes through the generic `PICKUP_TYPES.bomb` spawn path, and the held face's constants are the same values the spawned face uses.
3. **Given** a placed bomb, **When** its fuse expires, **Then** its blast consequences (blocks, enemies, player damage/knockback and the cosmetic explosion) and its removal are produced by the bomb module reached through the registry, not by a page branch naming the bomb kind.

### User Story 3 - The chest and the rope ladder share one interactable subtype, and the landing-row helper stays singular (Priority: P2)

The rope ladder bundle and the chest are the same kind of object: both are authored into the level, both sit inert until the player presses Up on them, and both change state when activated (the bundle unrolls; the chest opens). Today they are separate concerns: the ladder is unregistered live state, while the chest is the last "degenerate one-member family" — a singular `CHEST_TYPE` in `entities/chests/` whose type and state/constants live in two places (consumers import `CHEST_TYPE` from `entities/chests` and the state/helpers from `entities/Chest`) — and the analysis asks whether it should be promoted to a keyed registry or folded into another family. Separately, "first landing row below" was implemented three times (bomb, ladder, stalactite) and R-002 already consolidated all three onto `Standable.ts`'s `findLandingRow`, each caller passing its own predicate. After this feature the rope ladder and the chest are both `WorldInteractableType` entries reached through one import path (resolving X7 by folding, not by keeping a one-member family), and the shared landing scan remains the single home for every downward landing query.

**Why this priority**: It is the issue's X7 and P3. It settles both so R-008 leaves no "to be decided" family behind, and it removes the chest's duplicate import path.

**Independent Test**: Confirm the rope ladder and chest are both `WorldInteractableType` entries whose activation travels the shared interaction dispatch, that a consumer reaches the chest family from one place, and that the bomb and ladder still resolve their landing rows through the shared `findLandingRow` with their own predicates (no third per-kind scan). Confirm chest opening, the HUD counter and the completion screen are unchanged.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the chest, **When** its entry is inspected, **Then** it is a `WorldInteractableType` entry (no `step`) whose `onPlayerInteract` opens it and whose outcome carries the key cost and the fact reveal, and the chest family (state, helpers, constants and view) is reachable from a single import path with the two old paths (`entities/Chest` and `entities/chests`) no longer both existing.
2. **Given** a closed chest the player stands on with a key, **When** Up is pressed, **Then** the shared interaction dispatch opens it exactly as today; **Given** the same chest with no key, **Then** the existing "no key" hint is unchanged.
3. **Given** the placed bomb and the rope ladder, **When** each resolves the row it lands on, **Then** both go through the shared `Standable.findLandingRow` with their own solidity predicate, and no deployable-item module re-implements the downward scan.
4. **Given** an opened chest, **When** the level runs, **Then** its open state, the chests counter and the all-chests-open completion trigger behave exactly as today.

### User Story 4 - The migration is invisible in-game (Priority: P1)

This is a refactor of already-shipped behaviour, so the acceptance bar is that nothing the visitor sees changes: a placed bomb still falls under gravity, lights through its fuse frames and detonates with the same blast effect, damage and knockback; a bomb pot still drops the same bobbing bomb; a ladder bundle still unrolls over the same ~0.5 s into the same effective terrain; a deployed ladder still survives a death and rolls back on Reset Game; a chest still opens on the same trigger with the same key cost and completes the level; and the HUD counters, reset scopes and draw order are identical.

**Why this priority**: It is the acceptance bar for every refactor in this phase, and it is the verification story: it runs last, after US1–US3, over the combined refactor. A naive consolidation silently changes a timing rule (the fuse while falling, the unroll step cadence), a reset scope (a bomb cleared on death, a ladder or chest surviving one it should not), an interaction precedence (a chest opening when a ladder should deploy), a draw depth (a bomb or chest drawn above or below a block) or a landing rule (a bridge or crumbling floor treated differently).

**Independent Test**: Play a level and compare against the pre-refactor build: pick up a bomb pot's drop, place a bomb on flat ground and over a ledge, stand under a falling stalactite-free clue and watch the blast damage; deploy a ladder onto a bridge, a crumbling floor and a mushroom cap; open a chest with and without a key; die, respawn and use Reset Game between placements; finish the level. Separately unit-test each kind's step, landing predicate, removal, interaction outcome and draw dispatch.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a placed bomb, **When** it falls and detonates, **Then** its fall speed, landing row (including a bridge and an unbroken crumbling floor stopping it, and a ladder not stopping it), fuse frame timing, blast area, block/enemy/player consequences and removal are byte-identical to today.
2. **Given** a rope ladder bundle, **When** it is deployed, **Then** its unroll duration, step cadence, landing row, effective-terrain mutation and one-way lifecycle are identical to today, and a deployed ladder still survives death and is rolled back by Reset Game only.
3. **Given** a chest, **When** it is opened, **Then** its open trigger, key cost, fact reveal, open state, chests counter and all-chests-open completion are identical to today, and it survives death and is rolled back by Reset Game only.
4. **Given** a death/respawn, a Reset Game and an editor "Try", **When** they run, **Then** each kind's collection is cleared or rebuilt in exactly the scope it uses today.
5. **Given** the draw pipeline, **When** a frame renders, **Then** every object is drawn at exactly the depth it occupies today relative to terrain, ladders, pickups, blocks, hazards, crumbling floors and chests.
6. **Given** the existing placed-bomb, rope-ladder, bomb-pickup, chest, renderer and page tests, **When** they run after the change, **Then** their assertions are unchanged except for renames/import paths/hook signatures, and the production build succeeds.

### Edge Cases

- ✅ **The fuse advances while the bomb falls.** The bomb's `step` always advances the fuse even mid-air, and gravity pulls it toward its landing row; the shared tick must not gate the fuse on being landed, and must not hand the kind level state it does not use.
- ✅ **A falling bomb detonates before it lands, or falls out of the level.** The blast is centred on where the bomb actually is when the fuse expires (its current tile, not its placement tile); a bomb with no landing row (a bottomless column) is removed without exploding. Both rules belong to the bomb kind and must survive the move.
- ✅ **A ladder bundle on the bottom row is a zero-length landing, and a bridge stops the unroll.** The ladder's landing predicate differs from the bomb's (plain `isSolid`, including `bridge`) and must stay its own predicate passed to the shared scan; the two kinds must not be collapsed onto one "solid for a deployable item" rule.
- ✅ **A deployed ladder mutates the effective terrain, a placed bomb or chest never does.** The ladder feeds `activeLevel` (the grid physics reads) with `ropeLadder` cells; the bomb is non-solid and the chest is a trigger box, and neither enters the block placements. The registry must not force one terrain model on all kinds.
- ✅ **`step` is optional, so a one-shot kind fits the umbrella.** The rope ladder ticks (its unroll timer), while the chest is one-shot (closed → open, no per-tick state). The umbrella's `step` is optional so the chest can be a `WorldInteractableType` entry without inventing a no-op tick.
- ✅ **Interaction precedence is preserved.** The current page offers an Up press to a rope-ladder bundle before a chest, so the press is consumed by the deploy and never also opens a chest. The shared interaction dispatch must preserve that order (a per-kind interaction priority or an equivalent mechanism) and consume the press at most once.
- ✅ **The chest's activation has side effects beyond its own state.** Opening a chest consumes a key and reveals a fact; it must be expressed as a declarative interaction outcome the shared pass applies (mirroring `PickupOutcome`), not by the chest module importing state. The "standing on a closed chest with no key" hint must stay unchanged.
- ✅ **The kinds are drawn at different depths.** The ladder bundle/shaft draws at terrain level (before signs, pickups and blocks); the placed bomb draws after blocks; the chest draws at its current band. A single dispatch must be invoked at each band, mirroring how R-006's `drawPickups` uses a per-kind draw layer rather than moving everything into one pass.
- ✅ **The reset scopes are per kind.** A placed bomb is cleared by `resetGame()` (death/respawn); a rope ladder's and a chest's live state is rebuilt only by `resetGameProgress()` (Reset Game / editor Try / theme remount), so they survive a death. Sharing one collection must not merge the scopes.
- ✅ **The bomb pot's drop is a `PickupKind`, not a deployable-item kind.** The pot declares `drop: 'bomb'` and routes through `PICKUP_TYPES.bomb`; the registry must not capture the dropped-pickup path or create a second bomb spawn route.
- ✅ **`bombLandingRow`/`ropeLadderLandingRow` remain thin wrappers with a deliberate off-by-one.** The shared `findLandingRow` returns the first solid row strictly below; the bomb and ladder each subtract one, the stalactite does not. This mapping stays with each kind.

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

- **FR-001a**: A `DeployableItemType<S>` umbrella registry contract MUST exist for the level's player-affected objects. It MUST compose the shipped `WorldType<S>` contract (`contracts/WorldType.ts`) for its `key`/kind discriminator and `draw(state, dc)`, and MUST add a shared state base (`id`, `col`, `row`, world `x`/`y`, `kind`), a primary `sprite` descriptor, a draw layer, a `resetScope` and an optional `box(state)` world rect — `box` stays optional and the type MUST NOT require `Boxed<S>`.
- **FR-001b**: The umbrella MUST be specialised into two subtypes: `SpawnedType<S>` for objects the player spawns at runtime (a required `spawn`/creation entry point and a required `step`) and `WorldInteractableType<S>` for objects placed in the level and activated by the player (a required `onPlayerInteract`, a required `interactionPriority` that orders activation precedence, an optional `step`, an optional effective-terrain hook).
- **FR-001c**: The kind vocabulary MUST be the leaf union `'bomb' | 'ladder' | 'chest'` (the ladder's literal stays `'ladder'` even though its module is `RopeLadder.ts`). The placed bomb MUST be a `SpawnedType` entry; the rope ladder and the chest MUST be `WorldInteractableType` entries.
- **FR-001d**: The `DEPLOYABLE_ITEM_TYPES` registry MUST join the existing `entities/WorldType.test.ts` cross-registry conformance suite and MUST be added to the page's `collectSheetSources` discovery list so its sprites load without a hand-listed `loadImage` call.
- **FR-002**: Exactly ONE live `deployableItems` collection MUST exist, holding every live object of every kind and discriminating entries by kind. It MUST replace the separate `placedBombs`, `deployableLadderStates` and `chestStates` live arrays. The per-kind reset scopes MUST be preserved: placed bombs are cleared on death/respawn; rope ladder and chest live state is rebuilt only on a full progress reset (Reset Game / editor Try / theme remount), so they survive a death.
- **FR-003**: ONE shared tick MUST advance every live object that has a `step` once per `playing` tick, forwarding each entry to its kind's own `step`, and MUST run in the same tick phase and freeze window as the current paths (advancing during `playing`, frozen during pause/death). The "one tick" is two phase-ordered call sites: `tickDeployableItems` (early, pre-physics — the rope ladder's current early position in the frame, so a newly-deployed shaft is visible to the same tick's collision) and `applyDeployableItemConsequences` (late, post-physics, before the player state is persisted — the placed bomb's current late position, so a blast still damages the same tick's player state exactly as today). Each kind's late consequences MUST be applied through that kind's own registry hook at the late call site. The page MUST contain no per-kind step branch. The exact tick signature and the context passed to `step` are planning decisions bounded by this requirement and FR-010.
- **FR-004**: ONE `drawDeployableItems` dispatch MUST draw every object through its kind's own `draw`, and MUST be invoked at the named `drawLayer` bands needed to preserve today's per-kind order: `terrain` (the rope ladder's bundle/shaft at terrain level), `afterBlocks` (the placed bomb after blocks) and `afterCrumblingFloors` (the chest after crumbling floors). The page/Renderer MUST NOT keep a hand-wired per-kind draw call for a named kind. Whether the band comes from a per-kind `drawLayer` (mirroring `PickupType.drawLayer`) or an equivalent mechanism is a planning decision.
- **FR-005**: ONE shared interaction dispatch MUST offer a player activation press to `WorldInteractableType` entries and apply the first entry's outcome through one generic applier, preserving today's precedence (a rope-ladder bundle deploys before a chest opens) and consuming the press at most once. The page MUST contain no per-kind interaction branch. A kind's activation outcome (its new state, plus any key cost or fact reveal) MUST be returned as declarative data the shared applier applies, not by the kind importing state.
- **FR-006**: Each kind's own consequences MUST stay owned by that kind's module and be reached through the registry, not by a page branch on a kind name. For the bomb this covers the blast decision, its geometry and the bomb's removal: the module owns the fuse-expiry detonation trigger, the blast centre/area and the fall-out removal, and expresses them as a declarative `BlastRequest` (mirroring `PickupOutcome`) that the shared applier resolves against blocks, enemies and the player, plus the cosmetic explosion; for the rope ladder this covers the `rolled → deploying → deployed` lifecycle and the application of a deployed shaft to the effective terrain; for the chest this covers its open trigger, key cost and fact reveal. Late consequences MUST be invoked through the kind's registry hook at the position defined by FR-003, not by the shared state advance, and the shared applier — not the kind module — MUST perform the damage application. The exact hook shapes are planning decisions bounded by this requirement and FR-010.
- **FR-007**: The bomb concept MUST live in ONE home exposing both faces: its held/pickup face (registered in `PICKUP_TYPES` as `bomb`) and its spawned/placed face (registered as the bomb `SpawnedType`). That home MUST own the placed state machine, the fuse-frame mapping and timing, the fall/landing rules, the held pickup view and the shared sprite/constants. `engine/PlacedBomb.ts`'s placed state machine and `entities/pickups/Bomb.ts`'s pickup view MUST be consolidated into that home, and the previously folded `entities/BombPickup.ts` MUST NOT be reintroduced. The exact module path is a planning decision.
- **FR-008**: The held and spawned bomb faces MUST share one set of constants and one sprite so they cannot drift, and a destroyed bomb pot MUST still drop through the generic `PICKUP_TYPES.bomb` spawn path with its existing id/position convention. No second bomb spawn route and no duplicate constant set may exist.
- **FR-009**: The chest MUST fold into the `WorldInteractableType` subtype and the chest family (state, helpers, constants and view) MUST collapse behind ONE import path, so the two current paths (`entities/Chest` and `entities/chests`) no longer both exist. `ChestPlacement` gains `col`/`row` derived from the marker it already reads — a type-only change with no level/JSON data change. Chest-visible behaviour MUST be unchanged (open trigger, key cost, fact reveal, open state, chests counter, all-chests-open completion). Finding X7 is resolved by this fold — no one-member family is left behind.
- **FR-010**: The shared downward landing scan MUST remain `Standable.ts`'s single `findLandingRow`, with each kind supplying its own solidity predicate and its own off-by-one mapping. The placed bomb and the rope ladder MUST route through it (already true after R-002), and R-008 MUST NOT introduce a per-kind landing scan or collapse the two kinds' differing predicates onto one rule. The stateless `ladderBundle` tile interaction itself belongs to R-015, not here.
- **FR-011**: Player-visible behaviour MUST be preserved exactly — bomb placement/fall/fuse/detonation/blast and damage/knockback, ladder deploy/progress/landing/effective-terrain effect and one-way lifecycle, chest open/key/fact/completion, bomb-pot drops, bomb-pickup collection and capacity, HUD counters, reset scopes, interaction precedence and draw order MUST be unchanged. No gameplay, tuning, visual, level-data or translation change is allowed.
- **FR-012**: All existing tests MUST migrate and MUST pass; assertions MUST be unchanged wherever only a name, signature, module location or import path changed, and where a hand-wired path moved behind a registry the tests MUST assert the equivalent new form — never weakened, skipped or deleted. The production build MUST succeed.
- **FR-013**: No compatibility re-export, alias or second code path MAY preserve the old `placedBombs`/`deployableLadderStates`/`chestStates` split, the hand-wired page tick/draw/interaction passes, the old draw wrappers, the split bomb concept, or the chest's second import path. A read-only projection derived from `deployableItems` (e.g. the HUD/completion view of the chest entries) is permitted and is explicitly not a compatibility alias or a second code path.
- **FR-014**: The change MUST NOT widen R-001's forbidden edges: `contracts/` MUST stay a leaf, no new `level/ → engine/`, and no new `engine/ → state/`. The `deployableItems` collection, its tick, its draw and its interaction dispatch MUST stay state/page-layer wiring that the engine consumes, not engine modules importing state.

### Key Entities

- **`DeployableItemType<S>`**: the umbrella registry contract for the level's player-affected objects — it composes the shipped `WorldType<S>` (`key`, `draw`) and adds a shared base (a state with `id`/`col`/`row`/`x`/`y`/`kind`, a primary `sprite` descriptor, an optional `box(state)` (not the required `Boxed<S>`), a draw layer (`terrain`/`afterBlocks`/`afterCrumblingFloors`), a `resetScope` and the composed `draw(state, dc)`). Its `step`, `onPlayerInteract`, late-consequence and effective-terrain hooks are optional; the two subtypes require the ones their category needs.
- **`SpawnedType<S>`**: the subtype for objects the player spawns at runtime — requires a creation entry point and a `step`. Today's only entry is the placed bomb. Extensible to a thrown item, a placed turret or a bear trap.
- **`WorldInteractableType<S>`**: the subtype for objects placed in the level that the player activates — requires `onPlayerInteract` and a required `interactionPriority` (the activation-precedence mechanism: lower runs first), and allows an optional `step` and effective-terrain hook. Today's entries are the rope ladder (priority `0`, which ticks while unrolling) and the chest (priority `1`, one-shot, no `step`).
- **The `deployableItems` collection**: the single live store of every object, discriminated by kind, replacing `placedBombs` + `deployableLadderStates` + `chestStates`. Owns one shared tick, one shared draw dispatch and one shared interaction dispatch while keeping each kind's reset scope.
- **The bomb module (one home, two faces)**: owns the spawned/placed face (`SpawnedType` entry: state machine, fuse frames, fall/landing) and the held/pickup face (`PICKUP_TYPES.bomb` view), sharing one constants/sprite set. Replaces `engine/PlacedBomb.ts` + `entities/pickups/Bomb.ts`.
- **The rope-ladder entry**: the ladder's live state (`rolled`/`deploying`/`deployed`, landing row, step cadence) registered as a `WorldInteractableType` entry, feeding the effective-terrain application. Its author-seeded `ladderBundle` tile half stays R-015's.
- **The chest entry**: a `WorldInteractableType` entry (no `step`) whose `onPlayerInteract` opens it and whose outcome carries the key cost and fact reveal; the chest family (state, helpers, constants and view) collapses behind one import path.
- **`Standable.findLandingRow`**: the single shared downward landing scan; each kind supplies its own predicate and off-by-one mapping. Preserved, not rebuilt.

## Assumptions

- **`DeployableItemType<S>` composes the shipped `WorldType<S>` contract; `box` stays optional.** Every existing family registry (`PickupType`, `EnemyType`, `BlockType`, `ChestType`) composes `contracts/WorldType.ts`'s `WorldType<S>` (`key` + `draw`), and the deployable-item registry does the same so the family-interface pattern stays uniform and the existing `entities/WorldType.test.ts` conformance suite covers it. `box` is kept an optional member rather than composing `Boxed<S>`, because the non-solid bomb and the terrain rope ladder have no collision-rectangle consumer today (the chest does supply a box from its trigger); forcing a required `box` would invent consumers. Resolved in the 2026-09-26 clarification session.
- **The umbrella has two subtypes, split by how an object comes into play.** `SpawnedType<S>` is the runtime-spawned category (the player creates it — today the bomb) and `WorldInteractableType<S>` is the load-placed, player-activated category (today the rope ladder and the chest). This is the resolution of the earlier "where does the chest belong?" question: the rope ladder is author-seeded exactly like the chest, so both are `WorldInteractableType`, while the bomb is the only `SpawnedType` today. The umbrella keeps "one collection / one tick / one draw / one interaction" true while the subtypes keep the two categories distinct and extensible.
- **The chest folds into the world-interactable family; X7 is resolved by folding, not by keeping a one-member family.** The chest becomes a `WorldInteractableType` entry whose activation travels the same `onPlayerInteract` path as the ladder's deploy, and the chest family collapses behind one import path. This supersedes any "keep `CHEST_TYPE` singular and self-contained" reading: the family is no longer a degenerate one-member registry. Resolved in the 2026-09-26 clarification session.
- **Per-kind consequences stay with the kind; the registry unifies lifecycle, not damage or gameplay models.** The bomb's blast/removal, the ladder's terrain application and the chest's key/fact activation are legitimately kind-specific and are reached through the registry via narrow hooks (their exact shape is a planning decision). This phase does not extract a bomb-damage subsystem — that is R-011 (D6 `BombSystem`).
- **The one shared tick is two-phase in time to preserve both current orderings.** The single advance (`tickDeployableItems`) keeps the rope ladder's pre-physics site, so a deploy completes into `activeLevel` before the same tick's collision reads it; each kind's late consequence hook then runs at the bomb's post-physics site, so blast damage lands on the same tick's `next` player state before it is persisted. Collapsing both into one site would shift one kind's observable timing by a tick and break FR-011; the two-phase split is the resolution, and it is what "one tick" means here. Resolved in the 2026-09-26 clarification session.
- **The interaction dispatch preserves today's precedence.** A rolled rope-ladder bundle under the player consumes the Up press before a chest the player stands on can open, exactly as today. The mechanism (a per-kind interaction priority or an equivalent) is a planning decision; the precedence and the consume-at-most-once rule are requirements (FR-005).
- **The generic interaction outcome mirrors `PickupOutcome`.** A kind's activation returns declarative data — its new state plus any key cost or fact reveal — and the shared applier is the only writer, so the chest module needs no `entities/ → state/` edge and the pickup and interaction vocabularies stay consistent.
- **Placed-item sprites load through the registry, like every other family.** `DeployableItemType<S>` carries a primary `sprite: SpriteDescriptor` and `DEPLOYABLE_ITEM_TYPES` is added to the page's `collectSheetSources` walk, so a new kind that draws from an already-registered sheet or a sheet that is its own primary `sprite.sheet` needs no hand-listed `loadImage` line. This is what makes FR-001/SC-003's "one module plus one registry line" true for a kind with new art; it does not broaden R-008 into R-012's full `AssetLoader` (secondary-only sheets remain hand-listed, as they are for every family). Resolved in the 2026-09-26 clarification session.
- **The registry owns spawned and world-interactable objects; dropped pickups stay in their own family.** The analysis's open question ("do dropped pickups join the same registry as placed bombs/ladders, or stay in the pickup family?") is resolved as: `DeployableItemType` covers the placed objects, while dropped pickups keep the R-006 `Pickup`/`PICKUP_TYPES` lifecycle (collect-once, capacity, collision, per-kind stores). Dropped pickups already have the "one collection / one tick / one draw" shape the issue wants, but their collect-once semantics are a different lifecycle; forcing them into this registry would undo R-006. The exact boundary is confirmed by `PICKUP_TYPES.bomb` remaining the pot drop's destination.
- **Draw order is preserved through per-kind draw bands.** A single `drawDeployableItems` dispatch is invoked at the bands the kinds occupy today (ladder at terrain level, bomb after blocks, chest at its current band), mirroring R-006's `PickupType.drawLayer`, rather than moving every kind into one depth.
- **P3 is already satisfied and only preserved.** `Standable.findLandingRow` is the single downward scan with all three callers routed through it (R-002); R-008 keeps it as each kind's landing home and does not reintroduce a per-kind scan. The issue's "route the landing-row helper through `Standable`" is therefore a verification, not new work.
- **R-015 owns the stateless-tile half.** The rope ladder's rolled `ladderBundle` is a stateless tile until deployed. R-008 owns the live-object end and MUST NOT build a second tile registry; if the stateless-tile contract has not landed, R-008 leaves the bundle tile behaviour as-is and changes no tile semantics.
- **The `DeployableItemType` state base is the analysis's `GridObject<S>` runtime-and-interactable subset.** R-008 introduces only that subset (no shared `GridObject` interface and no `entities/` grid-object/actor reorganisation); the broader taxonomy split remains deferred per analysis §3.5.
- **Player-visible behaviour is preserved exactly; only the structure moves.** The sanctioned changes are the umbrella registry + subtypes, the single `deployableItems` collection/tick/draw/interaction, the bomb one-home consolidation, the rope-ladder module move/rename and the chest fold — never a change to tuning, timing, gates, blast area, landing rules, interaction precedence, draw depth, counter values or reset scope.
- **No data migration.** Shipped levels, markers, placements, sprites and tuning are unchanged; only TypeScript types, hooks and module homes move.
- **Layer invariants continue to hold.** `contracts/` stays a leaf; `entities/`/`engine/` depend down on it; `level/` never reaches into `engine/`; `engine/` never imports state (R-001). The `deployableItems` collection, its tick, its draw and its interaction dispatch stay state/page-layer wiring.
- **R-015 is a pending dependency, not a blocker for the bomb and chest halves.** The bomb consolidation, the chest fold and the umbrella registry can land independently of the tile registry; the rope ladder's stateless-bundle interaction is the only piece that consumes R-015, and it must not be rebuilt here.
